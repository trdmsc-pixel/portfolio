01

Director's Preface

Having read and studied the screenplay carefully, I want to begin by noting what this film truly is — a quiet, powerful, emotionally honest family story set in the heart of rural India. It does not need spectacle. It needs a director who understands that its power lies in Radha's eyes, in the relationship between a child and her dwarf bull Aristadeva, and in the moral clarity of a community protecting what it loves. That understanding shapes everything about how this film should be made — and what it will cost to make it well.

The approach I am proposing is a hybrid production model that combines practical on-location shooting with AI-assisted post-production enhancement. Real actors. Real animals. Real locations across rural India. The AI tools — Kling 3.0, Seedance 2.0, and Higgsfield — are deployed not to replace reality, but to elevate it: cinematic grading, atmospheric enhancement in the night sequences, and targeted VFX in the 30 to 45 shots that benefit from it. The result will not look like an "AI movie." It will look like a film made by filmmakers who knew exactly what they were doing.

Below, I present two production scenarios — a Conservative Budget (₹58 Lakhs) and a Comfortable Budget (₹90 Lakhs) — along with my honest recommendation on which path gives this film the future it deserves.

AI Technology Partnership Strategy

⚡ Strategic Opportunity

Turning Our Tools Into Co-Partners

The AI tools central to this production — Kling 3.0, Seedance 2.0, and Higgsfield — are not merely software subscriptions. They represent companies actively working to establish their platforms as credible, professional-grade filmmaking tools in the Indian market. We are in a position to offer them something they cannot buy through conventional marketing: a real, theatrically-released feature film built using their technology as a core production component.

Kling 3.0 Seedance 2.0 Higgsfield

The pitch to these companies is straightforward: We are producing the first AI-hybrid feature film in India targeting theatrical release. We are not creating a YouTube short or a demo reel. We are creating a case study that will be watched, reviewed, and discussed across the Indian film industry — the third-largest film market in the world. The template we establish here will be followed by thousands of independent Indian filmmakers. Their logo, their credit, and their name on this film is a gateway into that entire ecosystem.

What to ask for in the partnership:

(a) Tool access credits — free or heavily subsidised platform access for the full post-production period, worth approximately ₹3–5 Lakhs in the budget.

(b) Dedicated technical advisor — a specialist from their internal team assigned to our post-production pipeline. This is the real value: their own engineers and artists know platform capabilities and quality techniques that no public tutorial covers. This expert access alone can elevate every AI-enhanced frame in the film.

(c) Co-branding on promotional materials — their logo in the opening credits, press materials, and festival submissions. A low-cost ask for us; high-value visibility for them.

A successful partnership here could reduce the post-production AI tooling cost from ₹3–5 Lakhs to near zero — while simultaneously securing technical support that materially improves the film's final quality. This is a sponsorship worth pursuing before the project enters post-production.

Legal, Copyright & Compliance

What Is Already Protected — and What Still Needs to Be Done

The screenplay of Radha and the Great Rescue is already protected. Uplifting Cinema Pvt. Ltd. holds WGA registration number 1977016 and Indian Copyright Office diary number 2825/2019-CO/L. This is foundational and properly handled. What follows outlines the additional legal layer required once production begins — because the completed film as a creative work is a separate, independent copyright from the screenplay.

Already Secured: Script copyright — WGA Reg. 1977016  ·  Copyright Diary 2825/2019-CO/L  ·  Uplifting Cinema Pvt. Ltd. (Mumbai) as rights holder.
Film Copyright Registration
The completed film must be registered separately under the Indian Copyright Act as a cinematographic work. This is distinct from the script registration and should be filed within 60 days of final delivery.
Production & Directorial Agreement
A properly drafted agreement between the director and the production company covering IP ownership, revenue share, screen credit, and creative rights — executed before a single day of principal photography begins.
Actor Release Forms
All cast — including background artists and child actors (requiring additional parental consent forms) — must sign image and performance releases cleared for theatrical, OTT, and international distribution.
Location Agreements & NOCs
All filming locations — goshala, riverbank, villages, rural roads — require signed release agreements. Government or public locations need additional No Objection Certificates from the relevant authority.
CBFC Certification
Mandatory for any theatrical release in India. Given the film's family-friendly content — no violence, no adult material — a U certificate is the expected outcome. This is a marketing asset: it opens school screenings, family audiences, and OTT placements.
Music Licensing & E&O Insurance
All original compositions must be registered. Errors & Omissions (E&O) insurance is a non-negotiable requirement for acquisition by major OTT platforms (Netflix India, Amazon Prime, ZEE5, SonyLIV) and most international distributors. This is included in the Comfortable scenario only.

A Note on Marketing & Distribution

Digital Presence Is in the Budget. Theatrical P&A Is Not.

Both budget scenarios include a soft digital and social media marketing allocation — ₹6 Lakhs in the Conservative scenario and ₹10 Lakhs in the Comfortable scenario. This covers trailer creation, poster design, social media campaigns, a light PR push, and select film festival submissions. This is sufficient to build the film's identity online and begin audience awareness ahead of release.

However, it is important to state clearly: full-scale theatrical release marketing — Print & Advertising (P&A), multiplex negotiations, press junkets, wide Out-of-Home campaigns, aggregator fees, and national distribution logistics — constitutes a separate, additional budget that has not been projected here. That phase of the film's journey operates on an entirely different financial scale and requires its own dedicated planning, studio or aggregator partnerships, and strategic commitment.

The marketing numbers in this proposal are my best estimate for a smart, lean digital-first presence that serves the film well during its festival and pre-release window. Full-fledged theatrical and OTT marketing is a conversation I believe the production company is better positioned to plan and lead — and I trust that the right strategy and partnerships already exist, or will be developed, for that critical phase.

Director's Recommendation

My Recommendation: Go Comfortable

I want to be direct. The Conservative scenario at ₹58 Lakhs will get us through production — the film will be shot, edited, and delivered. But it will arrive at the finish line without the oxygen it needs to compete in the real world. The missing E&O insurance alone means no major OTT platform will acquire the film without a further investment. A mid-level AI operator means the very technology that makes this production viable gets underused. And a 35-day shoot for a script this rich in locations, animals, child-performance-dependent scenes, and rural coverage is operating on borrowed time from the first day.

The Comfortable scenario at ₹90 Lakhs delivers the same film — but finished, protected, and ready for every distribution window available to it: theatrical, OTT, festival circuit, and international sales. The additional ₹32 Lakhs is not extravagance. It is the difference between a film that exists and a film that competes.

My recommended budget range to approve:
₹ 85 Lakhs  to  ₹ 1 Crore This gives us the Comfortable production model with additional flexibility for AI company partnership development, any co-production conversation costs, and the legal and compliance requirements of a proper theatrical release. A film of this quality and this heart — produced at this price point using the most current filmmaking technology available — is a genuine opportunity. I believe it deserves to be made the right way.
